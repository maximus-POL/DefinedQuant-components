## What the licences cover

- **Code** is licensed Apache-2.0. You may use, modify, distribute and fork it, including

- **Documentation, explanations and visuals** are licensed CC BY 4.0, which requires attribution.

## What they do not cover

Neither licence grants rights to the names, logos or visual identity:

- **Defined Quant**
- **Defined Flow**
- the wordmarks, logos and the site's visual identity

Apache-2.0 § 6 explicitly withholds trademark rights, and that is intentional here.

## What you may do without asking

- State accurately that your project uses, is built on, or is compatible with Defined Quant
- Link to the project and cite component IDs and versions
- Say "fork of Defined Quant" for an unmodified fork, provided you do not imply endorsement
- Use the name in academic or journalistic writing about the project

## What requires permission

- Naming your product, service, company or domain in a way that includes or resembles
  **Defined Quant** or **Defined Flow**
- Using the logos or visual identity
- Implying endorsement, certification, affiliation, or that your fork is the official project
- Marketing a modified version under the project name

**A fork must be renamed if it is distributed as a product.** The name is how users know whose
validation policy applies to what they are installing — and validation policy is the whole
product. A fork with different rules under the same name defeats it.

## Why this matters more here than usual

This project's output is trust claims. If "Defined Quant" can mean two different validation
standards, the claims mean nothing. The trademark reservation exists to keep the name attached
to one evidence policy, not to restrict use of the code.
